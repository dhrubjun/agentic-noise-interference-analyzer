# V0.2 Engineering Specification

## Agentic Noise & Interference Analyzer

**Version:** V0.2  
**Status:** Complete  
**Purpose:** Noise and interference characterization for single-channel sampled signals

---

## 1. Objective

V0.2 extends the validated V0.1 signal-analysis foundation with deterministic measurements for characterizing spectral noise and interference.

The objective is:

> Given one sampled signal, calculate reproducible evidence describing whether its spectral content is broadband, narrowband, mixed, harmonic, or insufficiently characterized.

The analyzer must remain conservative.

It may report measured evidence such as:

- strong spectral lines;
- local noise floors;
- line-to-floor ratios;
- harmonic relationships;
- band-power distribution;
- occupied bandwidth.

It must not automatically identify the physical cause of those features.

---

## 2. Processing Principle

V0.2 follows:

```text
Measurement
    ↓
Detection
    ↓
Characterization
    ↓
Engineering interpretation
    ↓
Possible hypothesis
```

Source attribution remains outside the scope of V0.2.

---

## 3. Implemented Measurements

V0.2 adds:

- band power;
- fraction of total power per configured band;
- median PSD-based global noise-floor estimate;
- PSD percentiles;
- spectral flatness;
- spectral centroid;
- spectral spread;
- 90% occupied bandwidth;
- local noise-floor estimation around detected spectral lines;
- line-to-floor ratio;
- harmonic-family detection;
- missing-fundamental support;
- harmonic match fraction;
- consecutive harmonic-run validation.

---

## 4. Narrowband Characterization

Each detected spectral line may include:

```text
frequency
peak PSD level
local noise floor
line-to-floor ratio
local frequency neighbourhood
number of background bins
```

Local noise floors are preferred when evaluating individual spectral components.

A single global noise floor may be misleading for strongly coloured spectra.

---

## 5. Harmonic Analysis

Automatic harmonic detection uses:

- candidate fundamentals derived from detected peak spacings;
- frequency-resolution-aware matching;
- relative frequency tolerance;
- maximum absolute tolerance;
- minimum number of matches;
- minimum match fraction;
- minimum consecutive harmonic run.

This design was strengthened after real vibration data produced a false harmonic family when a fixed absolute tolerance was used.

A detected harmonic family is evidence of harmonic structure.

It is not proof of a physical source.

---

## 6. Spectral Characterization

Current labels are:

```text
broadband-dominant
tonal-dominant
mixed
harmonic-rich
undetermined
```

The rules are deliberately conservative.

Important configured thresholds include:

```text
broadband spectral-flatness threshold
tonal spectral-flatness threshold
strong line-to-floor threshold
minimum harmonic matches
harmonic matching tolerance
minimum harmonic match fraction
minimum consecutive harmonic matches
```

These thresholds are engineering configuration values, not universal physical constants.

---

## 7. Occupied Bandwidth

V0.2 calculates the frequency interval containing a configured fraction of integrated spectral power.

The default is:

```text
90%
```

The output includes:

```text
lower frequency edge
upper frequency edge
occupied bandwidth
power fraction
```

Occupied bandwidth is retained as measurement evidence.

It is not currently used directly as a classification threshold.

---

## 8. Synthetic Validation

End-to-end synthetic validation includes:

- white noise;
- pure tones;
- tone plus weak noise;
- tone plus stronger broadband noise;
- harmonic series;
- missing fundamental;
- unrelated tones;
- low-frequency dominated signals;
- high-frequency dominated signals;
- constant signals.

Validated relationships include:

```text
white noise
→ high spectral flatness
→ large occupied bandwidth

pure tone
→ low spectral flatness
→ very small occupied bandwidth

higher broadband noise amplitude
→ higher estimated noise floor

low-frequency tone
→ lower spectral centroid

high-frequency tone
→ higher spectral centroid

harmonic series
→ harmonic-family detection

unrelated tones
→ no convincing harmonic family
```

---

## 9. Real-World Validation

### Vibration measurement

A real vibration recording sampled at 1024 Hz was analyzed.

Observed behavior included:

- approximately 97.5% of spectral power below 50 Hz;
- multiple strong narrowband components;
- low spectral flatness;
- no convincing harmonic family after stricter validation;
- final characterization: `tonal-dominant`.

The first harmonic algorithm incorrectly proposed a low-frequency harmonic family.

Real-data validation exposed this false-positive risk.

The harmonic detector was subsequently strengthened using frequency-resolution-aware and relative tolerances together with family-completeness checks.

---

### Environmental audio

ESC-50 environmental recordings were also analyzed.

A keyboard-typing recording produced:

```text
low spectral flatness
no strong narrowband lines
no harmonic family
final label: undetermined
```

A wind recording produced:

```text
very low spectral flatness
no strong narrowband lines
no harmonic family
approximately 95% of power below 250 Hz
90% occupied bandwidth approximately 241 Hz
final label: undetermined
```

These experiments demonstrated that low spectral flatness alone does not imply a tonal signal.

Real-world broadband processes can also have strongly coloured spectra.

---

## 10. Important Limitations

V0.2 does not perform:

- automatic event detection;
- transient classification;
- multichannel analysis;
- coherence analysis;
- cross-correlation;
- source attribution;
- causal inference;
- physical source identification;
- noise suppression;
- machine learning;
- LLM-based DSP calculations;
- agentic tool selection.

Global noise-floor estimates may be misleading for strongly coloured signals.

Peak prominence remains signal-scale dependent.

Characterization thresholds are configurable engineering heuristics.

The `undetermined` label is intentional and should be preferred over unsupported classification.

---

## 11. Reproducibility

V0.2 preserves:

- input metadata;
- sampling information;
- DSP configuration;
- PSD settings;
- band-power ranges;
- noise-floor settings;
- narrowband settings;
- harmonic-analysis settings;
- characterization thresholds;
- numerical results;
- plots.

Unavailable measurements are stored as `null` rather than fabricated numerical values.

---

V0.2 is considered complete because:

- analysis configuration and results are reproducibly stored.
