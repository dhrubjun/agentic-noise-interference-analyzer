# V0.2 Engineering Specification

## Agentic Noise & Interference Analyzer

Version: V0.2  
Name: Noise & Interference Characterization  
Status: Proposed baseline  
Depends on: V0.1 Core Single-Channel Signal Analyzer

## 1. Objective

V0.2 extends the validated measurements from V0.1 into quantitative characterization of noise and interference.

The objective is:

> Given one validated sampled signal, quantify its spectral structure and provide numerical evidence describing whether its energy behaves primarily as broadband noise, narrowband interference, harmonic interference, or a mixture of these behaviours.

V0.2 remains a deterministic DSP system.

It does not use:

- LLMs
- Ollama
- machine learning
- agentic reasoning
- automatic source identification
- causal inference

The processing philosophy becomes:

```text
Signal
  ↓
Validated V0.1 measurements
  ↓
Additional DSP measurements
  ↓
Noise/interference features
  ↓
Structured characterization evidence
```

The analyzer measures signal properties.

It does not guess the physical source.

---

## 2. Relationship to V0.1

V0.1 answers questions such as:

```text
What is the RMS value?

What frequencies are present?

What does the PSD look like?

Where are the spectral peaks?

How does the spectrum change with time?
```

V0.2 begins answering:

```text
How much power lies inside a selected frequency band?

What is the estimated background spectral level?

How far above the background is a detected spectral line?

Does the spectrum behave more like broadband noise or a tonal signal?

Are several detected lines related harmonically?

Where is most spectral energy concentrated?
```

V0.2 therefore builds on V0.1 rather than replacing it.

---

## 3. Supported Input

V0.2 initially retains the V0.1 signal assumptions:

- single channel
- real-valued samples
- uniformly sampled data
- known sampling frequency
- offline processing

V0.2 characterization operates primarily on validated outputs from:

- PSD
- amplitude spectrum
- spectral peak detection

The original signal remains available where needed.

---

## 4. V0.2 Characterization Categories

V0.2 will initially characterize four broad spectral behaviours.

### 4.1 Broadband behaviour

Energy is distributed across a relatively broad frequency range.

Example:

```text
White Gaussian noise
```

V0.2 should measure properties that provide evidence for broadband behaviour.

It should not claim:

```text
This is thermal noise.
```

---

### 4.2 Narrowband or tonal behaviour

A substantial amount of energy is concentrated near one or more frequencies.

Example:

```text
Noise + strong 750 Hz sinusoid
```

V0.2 should measure:

- line frequency
- line magnitude or PSD
- nearby background level
- line-to-background ratio

---

### 4.3 Harmonic behaviour

Several spectral lines appear close to integer multiples of a possible fundamental.

Example:

```text
50 Hz
100 Hz
150 Hz
200 Hz
```

The analyzer may report:

```text
A harmonic family consistent with a fundamental near 50 Hz was detected.
```

It must not automatically conclude:

```text
This is mains interference.
```

---

### 4.4 Mixed behaviour

Many real measurements contain:

```text
broadband background
+
one or more narrowband lines
+
possibly harmonic components
```

V0.2 must support such mixed cases.

---

# 5. Band-Power Analysis

The analyzer shall calculate the signal power contained within a selected frequency range.

For a PSD Pxx(f), band power between f1 and f2 is:

```text
Pband = integral from f1 to f2 of Pxx(f) df
```

Numerically, integration will be performed over the corresponding PSD bins.

Example bands:

```text
0–100 Hz
100–1000 Hz
1000–5000 Hz
```

The analyzer must not hard-code these bands as universal engineering bands.

Users or later analysis layers should be able to specify them.

Outputs should include:

```text
lower_frequency_hz
upper_frequency_hz
band_power
fraction_of_total_power
```

Validation requirement:

For controlled signals, power should move between bands as expected when signal frequency or noise distribution changes.

---

# 6. Noise-Floor Estimation

A strong spectral line must not automatically determine the estimated noise floor.

Example:

```text
white noise
+
very strong 1 kHz sinusoid
```

The 1 kHz tone should appear as interference above the background.

The background estimator should therefore be robust against isolated spectral peaks.

Initial V0.2 approach:

```text
PSD
 ↓
Convert valid PSD values to dB
 ↓
Use robust statistical estimation
 ↓
Estimate representative background level
```

Candidate statistics include:

- median
- lower or central percentile statistics

The exact estimator will be selected experimentally and validated before becoming the V0.2 default.

The analyzer must preserve:

```text
estimation method
frequency range
excluded bins if any
result
units
```

---

# 7. Local Noise-Floor Estimation

For a detected spectral line, a local background estimate is usually more meaningful than a single global value.

For a line near frequency f0:

```text
left neighbourhood
      ↓
exclude peak region
      ↓
right neighbourhood
      ↓
estimate local background
```

This allows a measurement such as:

```text
Peak frequency: 1000 Hz
Peak PSD: -42 dB
Local background: -71 dB
Line-to-background ratio: 29 dB
```

The exclusion width and neighbourhood width must be explicit parameters.

---

# 8. Line-to-Noise-Floor Ratio

For a detected spectral line:

```text
LNR = peak_level_dB - local_background_level_dB
```

The output will be reported in dB.

For example:

```text
peak = -45 dB
background = -70 dB

LNR = 25 dB
```

This provides stronger evidence of a narrowband interferer than peak amplitude alone.

V0.2 should avoid calling this SNR unless the desired signal and noise components are explicitly known.

Preferred term:

```text
line-to-background ratio
```

or:

```text
line-to-noise-floor ratio
```

---

# 9. Spectral Flatness

Spectral flatness provides evidence about whether power is distributed broadly or concentrated into a small number of spectral components.

Conceptually:

```text
flat spectrum
→ spectral flatness approaches 1

highly tonal spectrum
→ spectral flatness approaches 0
```

The standard measure is:

```text
geometric mean of PSD
---------------------
arithmetic mean of PSD
```

Important:

Spectral flatness is evidence.

It is not a complete noise classifier.

A threshold such as:

```text
flatness > 0.5 = noise
```

must not be adopted without validation.

---

# 10. Spectral Centroid

Spectral centroid measures the centre of mass of spectral power.

Conceptually:

```text
centroid =
Σ frequency × spectral_power
-----------------------------
Σ spectral_power
```

Example:

A signal dominated by low-frequency energy should have a lower centroid than a signal dominated by high-frequency energy.

Units:

```text
Hz
```

---

# 11. Spectral Spread

Spectral spread measures how widely the spectral energy is distributed around the spectral centroid.

Conceptually:

```text
small spread
→ concentrated spectrum

large spread
→ energy distributed across a wider region
```

Units:

```text
Hz
```

Centroid and spread must be treated as descriptive measurements, not source identifiers.

---

# 12. PSD Percentiles

V0.2 should calculate useful statistics of PSD values.

Examples:

```text
10th percentile
25th percentile
50th percentile
75th percentile
90th percentile
```

These values help describe the spectral distribution and may later support:

- robust noise-floor estimation
- anomaly detection
- comparison between measurements

The implementation must explicitly document whether the percentile is calculated:

```text
in linear PSD

or

after conversion to dB
```

The two are not interchangeable.

---

# 13. Dominant Spectral Component Contribution

V0.2 should quantify how much of the observed spectral energy is associated with dominant narrowband regions.

Example:

```text
total measured spectral power = 1.0

power around dominant tone = 0.35

dominant contribution = 35%
```

The bandwidth used around the spectral peak must be recorded.

This helps distinguish:

```text
strong tone + weak background
```

from:

```text
weak tone + dominant broadband background
```

---

# 14. Harmonic-Family Analysis

Given detected spectral peaks, V0.2 should search for frequencies that are approximately integer multiples of a candidate fundamental.

Example:

```text
candidate fundamental = 50 Hz

observed:
50.1 Hz
99.9 Hz
150.2 Hz
199.8 Hz
```

Possible structured result:

```text
estimated fundamental: 50.0 Hz
matched harmonic orders: 1, 2, 3, 4
matched frequencies: ...
frequency errors: ...
```

A frequency tolerance must be specified.

Possible tolerance approaches include:

```text
fixed Hz tolerance

or

tolerance related to spectral resolution
```

The resolution-aware approach is preferred.

The analyzer must not assume that the strongest line is always the fundamental.

For example:

```text
100 Hz may be stronger than 50 Hz.
```

---

# 15. Spectral Characterization Summary

V0.2 may combine validated numerical features into a conservative characterization.

Initial labels may eventually include:

```text
broadband-dominant
tonal-dominant
mixed
harmonic-rich
undetermined
```

However, these labels should not be implemented until the underlying measurements have been validated.

The characterization must retain its supporting evidence.

Example:

```text
characterization:
    mixed

evidence:
    spectral_flatness: 0.31
    strongest_line_to_floor_db: 22.5
    dominant_line_fraction: 0.18
```

A label without numerical evidence is insufficient.

---

# 16. Required Structured Outputs

A future V0.2 result may contain:

```text
characterization
├── band_power
├── noise_floor
├── spectral_features
│   ├── flatness
│   ├── centroid_hz
│   ├── spread_hz
│   └── percentiles
├── narrowband_lines
│   ├── frequency_hz
│   ├── level
│   ├── local_floor
│   └── line_to_floor_db
├── harmonic_families
└── characterization_summary
```

These results should eventually be stored in JSON alongside V0.1 measurements.

# 17. Explicitly Out of Scope

V0.2 does not include:

- transient/event detection
- automatic anomaly detection
- multichannel analysis
- cross-correlation
- coherence
- transfer functions
- source localization
- causal inference
- machine learning
- LLM interpretation
- Ollama
- investigation agent
- MCP
- adaptive filtering
- noise cancellation
- automatic suppression
- real-time processing

Those belong to later versions.

# V0.2 Validation Matrix

## Reference Signal V2-01: White Gaussian Noise

Purpose:

Validate broadband spectral behaviour.

Known properties:

```text
zero mean approximately
broad spectral distribution
no deterministic narrowband tone
```

Expected observations:

```text
no persistent dominant harmonic family

spectral flatness relatively high

line-to-floor values relatively low compared with strong-tone cases
```

Pass criteria will be established statistically using fixed random seeds and repeated experiments.

---

## V2-02: Pure Sinusoid

Example:

```text
Fs = 10 kHz
f0 = 1 kHz
```

Purpose:

Validate tonal characterization.

Expected:

```text
dominant peak near 1 kHz
low spectral flatness
high concentration of spectral power around 1 kHz
small spectral spread relative to broadband noise
```

---

## V2-03: Tone + White Noise

Signal:

```text
1 kHz tone
+
Gaussian noise
```

Purpose:

Validate local noise-floor estimation and line-to-floor measurement.

Expected:

```text
tone near 1 kHz

noise floor remains representative of background

line-to-floor ratio > noise-only case
```

---

## V2-04: Increasing Tone Amplitude

Use identical noise realization.

Test tone amplitudes:

```text
A1 < A2 < A3
```

Expected:

```text
line-to-floor ratio increases monotonically
```

This is an important quantitative validation.

---

## V2-05: Increasing Noise Level

Keep tone amplitude constant.

Use:

```text
σ1 < σ2 < σ3
```

Expected:

```text
noise floor increases

line-to-floor ratio decreases
```

---

## V2-06: Two Separated Tones

Example:

```text
500 Hz
2000 Hz
```

Purpose:

Validate multiple narrowband interferers.

Expected:

```text
two distinct characterized spectral lines
```

No false harmonic relationship should be reported unless the harmonic criteria are satisfied.

---

## V2-07: Harmonic Series

Example:

```text
50 Hz
100 Hz
150 Hz
200 Hz
250 Hz
```

Purpose:

Validate harmonic-family detection.

Expected:

```text
candidate fundamental near 50 Hz

harmonic orders approximately:
1, 2, 3, 4, 5
```

---

## V2-08: Missing Fundamental

Example:

```text
100 Hz
150 Hz
200 Hz
250 Hz
```

Underlying harmonic spacing:

```text
50 Hz
```

Purpose:

Ensure harmonic analysis does not require the fundamental itself to be the strongest or even present.

Expected:

The algorithm should eventually be capable of reporting a possible fundamental spacing near 50 Hz while clearly indicating that the fundamental line itself was not detected.

---

## V2-09: Non-Harmonic Multiple Tones

Example:

```text
470 Hz
913 Hz
1720 Hz
```

Purpose:

Prevent false harmonic-family detection.

Expected:

```text
no strong harmonic family
```

---

## V2-10: Low-Frequency-Dominated Noise

Generate filtered or shaped noise with most power at low frequencies.

Expected:

```text
low spectral centroid
greater low-band power
```

---

## V2-11: High-Frequency-Dominated Noise

Expected:

```text
higher spectral centroid
greater high-band power
```

Relative comparison with V2-10 should be unambiguous.

---

## V2-12: Band-Power Controlled Signal

Example:

```text
300 Hz tone
+
3000 Hz tone
```

Use bands:

```text
0–1000 Hz
1000–5000 Hz
```

Expected:

Each component contributes to the correct band.

Changing either tone amplitude must produce the expected corresponding band-power change.

---

## V2-13: Strong Tone With Weak Broadband Background

Purpose:

Test robust global noise-floor estimation.

Expected:

Adding a single strong tone should not raise the estimated broadband floor dramatically.

This experiment will be central to selecting the default floor estimator.

---

## V2-14: Mixed Signal

Example:

```text
broadband noise
+
700 Hz tone
+
1400 Hz tone
+
2100 Hz tone
```

Expected evidence:

```text
broadband background present

strong narrowband components present

harmonic family near 700 Hz

mixed spectral behaviour
```

---

## V2-15: Constant Signal

Purpose:

Boundary-case behaviour.

Expected:

```text
no meaningful broadband characterization

no fabricated harmonic family

flatness computation handled safely
```

No divide-by-zero or log-of-zero failure should occur.

---

## V2-16: Very Low-Level Signal

Purpose:

Numerical stability.

Expected:

No invalid values caused solely by extremely small but finite PSD values.

Appropriate numerical floors should be used only where mathematically required.

---

# Validation Principles

Every V0.2 DSP quantity must satisfy at least one of these:

```text
analytical validation

controlled synthetic comparison

monotonic behaviour test

independent numerical calculation

boundary-condition test
```

A visually plausible graph is not sufficient evidence of correctness.

---

# Core V0.2 Rule

The analyzer may say:

```text
A narrow spectral line exists near 50 Hz.

Its level is 24 dB above the estimated local background.

Additional lines occur near integer multiples of 50 Hz.
```

The analyzer must not yet say:

```text
The noise comes from the electrical mains.
```

That distinction will become important when the investigation agent is added later.
